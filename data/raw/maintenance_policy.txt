# ForgeIQ Demo Facility

# Maintenance Management Policy

Document ID: FIQ-POL-001
Revision: 1.0
Document Type: Maintenance Policy
Status: Fictional document for ForgeIQ development and testing

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines fictional examples of maintenance responsibilities, documentation practices, and escalation principles for the ForgeIQ demonstration environment.

It is intended to test the assistant's ability to retrieve organizational requirements and distinguish policy statements from technical specifications.

This document is not an approved policy for any real facility. Actual maintenance activities must follow the applicable organization's approved procedures, manufacturer documentation, and safety requirements.

## 2. Maintenance Responsibilities

### 2.1 Operators

Operators may report abnormal machine behavior and provide relevant information about operating conditions.

A useful report should describe the observed symptom, the approximate time of occurrence, the affected equipment, and any displayed alarms.

Operators must not perform maintenance activities beyond their authorization and training.

### 2.2 Maintenance Technicians

Authorized maintenance technicians are responsible for documenting assigned maintenance activities and recording relevant observations and findings.

Records should distinguish between:

* Reported symptoms.
* Directly observed conditions.
* Suspected causes.
* Confirmed findings.
* Corrective actions performed.
* Verification outcomes.

A suspected cause must not be recorded as a confirmed diagnosis without supporting evidence.

### 2.3 Engineering Personnel

Appropriately qualified engineering personnel may be involved when a fault requires further technical investigation, when recurring abnormalities need analysis, or when an engineering assessment is required.

The involvement of engineering personnel does not remove the need to follow approved safety and authorization requirements.

## 3. Maintenance Record Requirements

A maintenance record should contain, where applicable:

* Equipment identifier.
* Work order or record identifier.
* Date and time.
* Reporting person's identifier.
* Reported symptom.
* Observed equipment condition.
* Relevant readings and alarm messages.
* Inspection or diagnostic activities performed.
* Findings and their supporting evidence.
* Corrective actions.
* Replacement parts, if applicable.
* Responsible personnel.
* Verification outcome.
* Escalation details, if applicable.

Information that is unavailable should be marked as unknown or not recorded, as appropriate.

Records must not contain invented readings, unverified diagnoses, or undocumented claims that an activity was completed.

## 4. Fault Classification

For demonstration purposes, maintenance reports may be grouped into the following categories.

### 4.1 Routine Observation

A recorded condition that requires documentation or assessment but has not been established as a fault.

Example: An operator reports an unusual sound without a confirmed cause.

### 4.2 Abnormal Condition

An observation or indication that requires assessment under the applicable maintenance process.

Example: A displayed temperature reading differs from the relevant approved process specification.

### 4.3 Unresolved Fault

A fault whose cause or appropriate corrective action has not yet been established.

Example: A recurring machine interruption remains unexplained after the initial assessment.

### 4.4 Safety-Related Concern

A condition that may affect personnel safety or equipment integrity.

Safety-related concerns must be handled under the applicable approved safety and escalation procedures. This policy does not define a complete safety classification system.

## 5. Escalation Requirements

An issue should be escalated through the appropriate approved process when:

* The cause of an abnormal condition remains unexplained.
* A fault recurs after a documented corrective action.
* Available documentation does not establish the required specification or procedure.
* A condition may affect personnel safety or equipment integrity.
* Additional technical authority or specialist assessment is required.

The escalation record should identify the issue, the available evidence, the actions already taken, the unresolved questions, and the person or function receiving the escalation.

ForgeIQ must not invent an escalation recipient or claim that an escalation has occurred unless the available records support that statement.

## 6. Document Authority and Conflicts

Different documents serve different purposes.

A maintenance manual provides general equipment information. A technical datasheet may provide component specifications. An inspection checklist describes items to observe. A work order records a particular maintenance event. A policy describes organizational requirements.

These documents should not be treated as interchangeable.

If documents appear to conflict, ForgeIQ should identify the conflicting statements and their sources rather than silently selecting one as correct.

The assistant must not assume that the newest-looking document is approved or authoritative unless its revision and approval status have been established.

Unresolved conflicts must be referred to the appropriate qualified personnel or approved document-control process.

## 7. Role of ForgeIQ

ForgeIQ is a document retrieval and question-answering assistant.

It may:

* Locate relevant passages in the available knowledge base.
* Summarize documented requirements.
* Identify the source supporting an answer.
* Explain when requested information is missing.
* Highlight conflicting statements for further review.

It must not:

* Replace qualified engineering judgment.
* Invent equipment specifications or maintenance records.
* Certify equipment as safe.
* Claim that an inspection or maintenance activity was performed without evidence.
* Authorize equipment operation or maintenance.
* Present fictional demonstration documents as approved industrial procedures.
