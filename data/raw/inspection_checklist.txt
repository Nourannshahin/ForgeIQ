# Injection-Molding Machine Inspection Checklist

Document ID: FIQ-IC-001
Revision: 1.0
Document Type: Inspection Checklist
Status: Fictional document for ForgeIQ development and testing

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This checklist provides a fictional example of how routine inspection observations may be organized for an injection-molding machine.

It is designed to test ForgeIQ's ability to retrieve checklist items, identify relevant machine subsystems, and explain recording requirements.

This is a synthetic demonstration document, not an approved inspection checklist. It does not authorize work on real equipment or replace manufacturer instructions, site procedures, or applicable safety requirements.

## 2. Equipment Identification

Equipment identifier: FIQ-IMM-001

Equipment description: Fictional injection-molding machine

Inspection record identifier: To be assigned for each inspection.

Inspection date and time: To be recorded by the authorized inspector.

Inspector identifier: To be recorded by the authorized inspector.

## 3. General Machine Condition

Example inspection observations include:

* Record any visible external damage.
* Record unusual sounds or vibrations observed during authorized operation.
* Record unexpected alarms displayed by the machine control system.
* Record visible signs of leakage or contamination.
* Record any abnormal condition reported by the operator.

For each observation, record the affected area, the observed condition, and any relevant supporting information.

An observation alone does not establish a confirmed root cause.

## 4. Hydraulic System Observations

Example observation categories include:

* Visible hydraulic fluid leakage.
* Apparent changes in hydraulic fluid condition, where assessment is authorized.
* Unusual hydraulic system noise.
* Abnormal temperature indications.
* Relevant alarms or reported changes in machine behavior.

Record the observation, its location where identifiable, the time it was noticed, and any associated symptoms.

Do not touch, loosen, or attempt to repair pressurized hydraulic connections as an informal inspection activity.

Hydraulic inspection and maintenance must follow approved isolation and safety procedures.

## 5. Temperature Control System Observations

Example observation categories include:

* Displayed temperature readings for relevant machine zones.
* Temperature alarms or abnormal indications.
* Differences between observed readings and the applicable approved process specification.
* Reported instability in temperature regulation.

Record the affected zone, displayed reading, time of observation, and relevant alarm information.

This checklist does not establish acceptable temperature ranges or authorize changes to temperature settings.

## 6. Machine Control System Observations

Example observation categories include:

* Active alarm messages.
* Unexpected status indicators.
* Missing or inconsistent displayed sensor readings.
* Reported interruptions in normal machine operation.

Record the exact displayed alarm text where available, the affected subsystem, the time of occurrence, and the machine state when the observation was made.

Do not assume that an alarm message alone identifies the underlying cause.

## 7. Inspection Record Requirements

A completed demonstration inspection record should contain:

* Equipment identifier.
* Inspection record identifier.
* Inspection date and time.
* Inspector identifier.
* Subsystem inspected.
* Checklist item or observation category.
* Recorded observation.
* Supporting readings or alarm messages, if available.
* Follow-up action or escalation, if applicable.
* Verification outcome, when follow-up is completed.

If an item cannot be inspected, record it as not inspected rather than assuming that it passed.

If information is unavailable, record it as unknown or not recorded rather than inventing a value.

## 8. Recording and Escalation

Observed abnormalities should be documented and handled according to the applicable approved site procedures.

Conditions that may affect personnel safety, equipment integrity, or reliable operation should be escalated through the appropriate maintenance process.

An inspector's observation, a suspected cause, and a confirmed diagnosis must be recorded as distinct types of information.

ForgeIQ may retrieve and summarize checklist requirements, but it must not certify equipment as safe or authorize operation based solely on this document.
