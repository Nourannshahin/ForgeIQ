# ForgeIQ Demo Facility

# Equipment Troubleshooting Guide

Document ID: FIQ-TSG-001
Revision: 1.0
Document Type: Troubleshooting Guide
Status: Fictional document for ForgeIQ development and testing

## 1. Purpose

This guide provides general examples of equipment symptoms, possible contributing factors, and information that maintenance personnel may need to investigate.

The guide is intended for testing the ForgeIQ document retrieval system. It is not an approved troubleshooting procedure for real industrial equipment.

Symptoms do not establish a confirmed root cause. Actual diagnosis must follow the equipment manufacturer's documentation and the site's approved maintenance and safety procedures.

## 2. Abnormal Operating Temperature

Abnormally high or unstable operating temperatures may indicate a problem requiring further investigation.

Possible contributing factors include restricted cooling, inadequate temperature regulation, a faulty temperature sensor, or operating conditions outside the approved process specification.

Relevant information to record includes the affected machine zone, observed temperature, time of occurrence, operating conditions, and any associated alarms.

Temperature readings should be compared with the approved specifications for the particular machine, material, and process.

This guide does not establish universal temperature limits or authorize adjustments to temperature settings.

## 3. Unusual Hydraulic System Noise

Unusual noise from a hydraulic system may indicate a condition requiring investigation.

Possible contributing factors include abnormal fluid conditions, air entering the hydraulic circuit, a developing mechanical problem, or changes in operating conditions.

Relevant observations include the type of noise, when it occurs, whether it is intermittent or continuous, any visible leakage, and any associated alarms or performance changes.

Maintenance personnel must not assume that noise alone identifies a specific failed component.

Hydraulic equipment may contain hazardous stored energy. Investigation and maintenance must follow approved isolation and safety procedures.

## 4. Hydraulic Leakage

Visible hydraulic fluid leakage should be documented and assessed according to the applicable equipment documentation and site procedures.

Useful observations include the apparent location of the leakage, when it was first noticed, whether the condition is recurring, and whether other abnormal symptoms are present.

Personnel must not touch, loosen, or attempt to repair pressurized hydraulic connections as an informal diagnostic check.

Suspected pressure-related hazards require appropriate isolation and assessment by qualified personnel.

This guide does not authorize continued operation of equipment with a suspected safety-critical fault.

## 5. Inconsistent Machine Operation

Inconsistent machine operation may be associated with changes in operating conditions, control-system alarms, sensor readings, or mechanical and electrical abnormalities.

Useful information includes the machine operating state, the sequence of events before the symptom occurred, active alarm messages, relevant sensor readings, and whether the symptom is repeatable.

Recorded observations should distinguish between what was directly observed and what is only a suspected cause.

If the cause remains unexplained, the issue should be escalated through the approved maintenance process.

## 6. Diagnostic Information and Records

A useful troubleshooting record should include:

* Equipment identifier.
* Date and time of the reported symptom.
* Description of the observed abnormality.
* Relevant sensor readings and operating conditions.
* Alarm messages, if present.
* Checks performed by authorized personnel.
* Findings and confirmed causes, if established.
* Corrective actions and verification results.
* Responsible personnel and escalation details.

A suspected cause must not be recorded as confirmed unless supported by appropriate evidence.

## 7. Escalation and Safety

Abnormalities that may affect personnel safety, equipment integrity, or production must be handled according to the site's approved procedures.

Unexplained or recurring faults should be escalated to appropriately qualified maintenance or engineering personnel.

ForgeIQ may help locate relevant documentation and summarize recorded information. It must not replace qualified diagnosis, approved operating procedures, or equipment-specific safety instructions.
